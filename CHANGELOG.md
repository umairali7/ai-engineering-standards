# Changelog

All notable changes to the AI Engineering Standards (AIES) project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html) for the standard as a whole (see [GOVERNANCE.md §6](GOVERNANCE.md#6-releases)). Individual documents carry their own version and status per the [Shared conventions](Shared/README.md).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Assessment calibration — scenarios as measurement instruments** ([platform/CALIBRATION.md](platform/CALIBRATION.md)): the project's dominant abstraction has matured from *assessment* → *evidence* → *instrument*, and the tooling now makes that explicit. An optional, schema-validated `calibration:` block on each scenario records **why it exists and how well it measures** — `objective`, a **required `ceiling_anchor`** ("what a 4 does that a 3 doesn't" — the load-bearing design-time criterion), `floor_anchor`, `gaming_rationale`, `expected_refusal`, an optional `hold_out_twin`, and an `empirical_status` with an **honesty guard** (nothing can claim `empirically_calibrated` before `design_reviewed`, and no scenario is empirically calibrated until a real-model panel exists). `aies suites calibrate` reports how far each area has progressed as an instrument (metadata, ceiling anchors, floor traps, refuse/escalate cases, hold-out twins, maturity). CALIBRATION.md defines the two phases (**design-time** vs **empirical**), the maturity ladder (Draft → Design-time calibrated → Empirically calibrated → Normative), and keeps three notions of quality distinct — **Calibration** (does each scenario measure well?), **Coverage** (are we measuring the right things?), **Conformance** (does every engine decide the same?). The **ceiling philosophy is outcome-based** (a Level-4 answer *protects the objective under test AND achieves the legitimate goal through a durable, governed path* — governance is a mechanism, not escalation-as-reflex). First area complete + deepened: **all 20 CA-07 (security & privacy) scenarios** are design-time calibrated with genuine ceiling anchors, 8 hold-out twins on the gameable pairs, and honest `empirical_status` (0 empirically calibrated — no model panel yet). The **coverage review** of the `security` assessment surfaced an RT3 depth gap and drove four new **behaviorally-distinct** high-tier scenarios (secrets-management, supply-chain, operational-resilience under a live incident, and delegated-authority governance), lifting CA-07's RT3/RT4 corpus from 5 to 9 across 9 distinct decision kinds. Coverage methodology now has **five lenses** (representation, weighting, overlap via the label-removal test, risk-tier depth, and **behavioral diversity**), frames depth as *effective independent evidence* (distinct diversity × independent observations × calibration quality) rather than raw item count, and `aies suites calibrate` reports distinct families / high-tier families per area so a thin behavioral spread is visible even when the tier count looks healthy. Tier maturity is stated honestly (RT2 decisional; RT3 operationally ready, expanded corpus required for decisional confidence). The coverage-driven pass then extended to the **`security` assessment's other mandatory areas**: **CA-12 (Governance, Risk & AI Safety)** and **CA-01 (AI-Native SDLC Foundations)** are now fully design-time calibrated (all scenarios, outcome-based ceilings), each deepened with new behaviorally-distinct high-tier scenarios — CA-12 gained autonomy-grant-validity-under-model-change (RT3), safety-guardrail integrity (RT4), and blast-radius re-tiering (RT3); CA-01 gained irreversible-money-movement tiering (RT3), unreconstructable-provenance-on-a-safety-system (RT4), and autonomy-under-delayed-failure (RT3). Across the three security-mandatory areas: CA-07 9 high-tier decision-kinds, CA-12 7, CA-01 5 (RT3/RT4 counts 9/8/7). The calibration pass then completed the **`coder` assessment** (RT2): CA-05 (AI-assisted implementation), CA-06 (testing, quality & evaluation), and CA-04 (architecture & solution design) are now fully design-time calibrated with outcome-based ceilings — for coding the ceiling is edge-cases/invariants/no-regressions and, for refactors, *exact* observable-behavior preservation; for testing it is spec-derived tests, boundary/negative coverage, and seeing through masked verification (including calibrating an LLM-as-judge, `SC-CA06-012`); for architecture it is genuinely-distinct options, the dominant failure mode, real ADRs, and refusing to assert unreviewable specifics. **106 of 199 scenarios are now design-time calibrated** (the `coder` and `security` assessments' areas: CA-01/04/05/06/07/12), 0 empirically calibrated (no model panel yet); all 199 validate.

- **[QUICKSTART.md](QUICKSTART.md) — the whole workflow in 5 minutes**, fully offline: `make demo` (one command) or a correct, copy-paste step-by-step (discover → `qualify --assessment coder --judge` → `assessment result` → `capabilities` → `conform engine`). Fixes the onboarding gap for new contributors. Also closed a real CI-enforcement gap: the `conformance/**` corpus (the arbiter, outside `platform/`) now triggers the conformance gate, so a change to what conformance *means* re-runs the check.

- **v1.0 Architecture Freeze + conformance suite + read-only API** (making AIES a dependable, buildable-against platform): (1) **Governance** — [STABILITY.md](STABILITY.md) freezes the contracts pinned to versions (RuntimeAdapter v1.0, assessment/profile/evidence/result schemas, decision semantics 1.0, renderer v1.0), declares **normative vs reference** and **reference-implementation vs conformance-suite**; [COMPATIBILITY.md](COMPATIBILITY.md) sets the evolution policy (additive-only, ADR-for-breaking, deprecation window) **enforced by CI**; [CONFORMANCE-POLICY.md](CONFORMANCE-POLICY.md) defines what "AIES Conformant" means per target. The ADR template gains a mandatory *Existing-Contracts Check*. (2) **Golden Evidence Package corpus + conformance runner** — `conformance/corpus/` is the data-first *arbiter*: immutable evidence packages + expected outcomes covering every outcome, reason kind, and load-bearing invariant. `aies conform engine` verifies any decision engine (the reference one, or a third-party one via a `decide_fn`) against it; CI-gated. (3) **Normative decision semantics in AESQS** — [AIES-AESQS-CS-01 §8](AESQS/capability-scoring.md) (R18–R21) makes the assessment outcome rules, no-compensation/no-blended-score, and the engine-version vs decision-semantics-version split part of the standard, not just the implementation. (4) **Thin read-only REST API** — `aies serve` exposes deployments, runs, evidence packages, Canonical Results, and conformance as JSON over stdlib `http.server` (no new dependency); a *consumer, not a decider* (serves stored results verbatim, `POST` refused). (5) **[REFERENCE.md](platform/REFERENCE.md)** — the complete vocabulary + artifact schemas + every command. The offline `make demo` now also shows per-area capabilities and the conformance check.

- **Versioned artifacts + reproducibility hardening** (turning AIES into a dependable, long-lived platform — v1.0 posture): (1) **Profiles are now versioned** — every profile carries a semver `version`, validated on load; the version used is **captured at run time** (immutable) into the evidence package and the Canonical Assessment Result, so a later edit to a profile file can never silently reinterpret a past certification. (2) **Both artifact envelopes are versioned** — the Evidence Package (`evidence_schema`) and the Canonical Assessment Result (`result_schema`) carry field-append-only schema versions; the Evidence Package is a **first-class, independently-versioned artifact** that can outlive a decision engine and be **replayed** through a future one (one Evidence Package → many Results, one per engine/semantics version). (3) **Engine vs semantics version split** — the result records `decision_engine_version` (which software decided) separately from `decision_semantics_version` (which normative AESQS decision policy was applied); a bugfix rebuild is never conflated with a policy change. (4) **`make demo`** — the entire workflow runs **fully offline** against the mock runtime (discover → assessment → qualify → auto-score → decide → render), gated in CI as `tests/test_demo.py`: executable documentation. The **mock adapter is now judge-aware** (emits deterministic, parseable rubric scores), so auto-scoring and the assessment decision run with no model, no key, no network. `make integration-demo` runs the same path against a real deployment (opt-in). `python -m aies` now works alongside the `aies` console script.

- **Assessment-as-Code — `aies qualify --assessment <name>` + `aies assessment`** ([ADR-0005](adr/ADR-0005-Assessment-as-Code.md)): a named qualification (enterprise, coder, security, architecture) is now **declarative data** — an `assessments/*.yaml` selecting the competency set, mandatory vs advisory, weights, EV-weighting profile, and sampling. Adding one is `git add`, not a code change; the engine hard-codes no assessment. A strict allowed-key **validator** enforces the declarative/engine-owned boundary (an author cannot add gates/minimums/decision rules) plus semantic consistency. The **Decision Engine** (frozen, engine-owned) turns evidence + the assessment into an authoritative outcome — **`PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE`**, decided over *mandatory* competencies with precedence `FAIL > INCONCLUSIVE > INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE > PASS` — with **structured reasons**; advisory competencies and all numeric/weighted figures are diagnostic/informational and **never decide** (no blended "82/100" leaderboard number; a strong area never offsets a failing one). Results are the **Canonical Assessment Result** `{metadata, evidence, decisions, diagnostics, analytics}` with immutable execution metadata (assessment id/version/schema, profile, platform+AIES version, fingerprints, timestamp) for reproducibility; renderers are views of it. Decisions read only evidence, so a run **re-decides without re-running inference**. New commands: `aies assessment list/show/validate/result` (with `--format html` for a self-contained, theme-aware, print-ready view of the Canonical Assessment Result), `qualify --assessment`. Shipped assessments are validated by `aies suites validate` — the same gate CI runs — so a broken assessment file cannot merge silently. New guide [ASSESSMENTS.md (AIES-PLAT-08)](platform/ASSESSMENTS.md).

- **Finish the in-progress items** (completing what was partially done): (1) **`aies qualify --resume-collection <run>`** fills only the *missing* responses of a partially-collected run (e.g. after an endpoint failure) and rebuilds the scoresheet — no re-collecting what succeeded; refuses if a suite changed since collection. (2) **`aies export <run>`** writes a run's prompts + responses + scores to a generic eval-log JSON that **round-trips through `aies import`** — finishing the ecosystem bridge (import + export). (3) The **evidence report** gained a **Grant Readiness** summary (per-area verdict + overall ready/blocked) and a **Residual risk** section (judge-produced, non-decisional, thin-margin gates). (4) **Signature verification is now functional** — `aies deployment verify-artifact` verifies a real detached Ed25519/RSA/ECDSA signature with the optional `crypto` extra (`pip install -e ".[crypto]"`), tested end to end (verify + tamper-fail). (5) **Dogfooding**: added `AGENTS.md` (AI-participation norms — also the CA-11 context asset), and CI now runs `aies audit .. --gate --rt 2` on the repo itself (which passes).
- **Complex scenario expansion (competency suites 11 → ~16 per area, 132 → 189 total)**: ~57 new high-complexity, distinct scenarios across all twelve competency areas — RT2 distinct prompts roughly 7 → 10 per area, plus more RT3 and RT4 (refuse/escalate-correct) cases. This directly reduces the run *repetition* that came from a decisional sample (30/50/100 items) drawing on too few distinct prompts, and deepens coverage with realistic multi-constraint, adversarial, and cross-cutting situations (e.g. lifecycle privacy review, stale-grant re-tiering, guardrail-downgrade refusal). All pass `aies suites validate` (0 errors). Authored via a parallel per-area workflow.
- **Repository conformance audit — `aies audit <repo>`** ([ADR-0004](adr/ADR-0004-Repository-Conformance-Audit.md)): a new axis — audits the *engineering practice evidenced in a repository* (not a model) against the twelve competency areas, scoring **maturity ML0–ML4** per area with **three-state evidence** (verified / asserted / gap) that is never false-green — absence of a signal is always a gap. Auto-detects the strong-signal areas (CA-01/05/06/07/08/11: conformance/ADRs/risk policy, AI-change provenance, tests + CI test gate + coverage, dependency manifests/locks/secret-scanning/`.env`-ignored/SECURITY, CI-CD/SBOM/signing, versioned context assets), presence-checks CA-04/09/10/12, and takes attestation (`--attest`, with evidence pointers) for the non-detectable CA-02/03. Modes: advisory scorecard + ranked recommendations (markdown/json), and `--gate --rt N` (non-zero exit if RT-required evidence is missing; an assertion can't stand in for a detectable control). Checks are pluggable and map to external standards (OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, CISA/NCSC, SLSA, ISO/IEC 42119). This makes [CONFORMANCE.md](docs/CONFORMANCE.md)'s claim-based model *executable*.

- **Import external eval results — `aies import <run> <file>`**: ingest EV1–EV6 scores produced by another tool (a custom Inspect/DeepEval task, a second judge, an offline pipeline) into a run as **automated**-kind ratings, subject to the same calibration gate as any rater. Generic JSON schema (`{source?, items:[{scenario_id, repeat?, scores:{EV1..EV6}, findings?}]}`); items that aren't six integers 0–4 are skipped and reported, never fabricated. Lets AIES complement the eval ecosystem rather than duplicate it (a single-metric benchmark is corroboration, not a six-dimension substitute).
- **Verify supply-chain provenance — `aies deployment verify-artifact <id> --artifact <path>`**: recompute a local artifact's SHA-256 and compare it to the declared `provenance.checksum` (exact), and verify a detached signature if `--pubkey`/`--signature` are given (best-effort via `cryptography`; Sigstore/cosign externally). A declared-but-unchecked signature is reported honestly, never a false pass — completing the model-signing/AI-BOM story from *declaration* to *verification*. `aies help` command tree/epilog restored to alphabetical and updated for `import`, `capabilities`, and `verify-artifact`.

- **Model-signing & AI-BOM provenance in deployment manifests**: a deployment MAY declare `provenance.signature` (e.g. OpenSSF Model Signing / Sigstore reference) and `provenance.ai_bom` (CycloneDX / SPDX reference or path). These are validated if present, surfaced in `aies deployment inspect`, and carried through the run manifest into the evidence package and Qualification Record — so an audit trail is complete against AI supply-chain standards (CROSSWALK §3b). Verification is delegated to the signer/registry, exactly like the artifact `checksum` (the platform records provenance; it does not yet cryptographically verify signatures).
- **Security & supply-chain crosswalk + AI impact assessment** (from the standards-landscape review): CROSSWALK §3b maps **CA-07/CA-08** to OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025), OWASP Agentic Top 10 (2026), MITRE ATLAS, NIST AI 100-2e2025, CISA/NCSC secure-AI guidance, and the model-supply-chain standards (OpenSSF Model Signing, AI-BOM, SLSA); KA-07 references gain ATLAS + the NIST adversarial-ML taxonomy + CISA/NCSC. New normative requirement **AIES-AEOS-GOV-01-R27** requires an **AI system impact assessment** (ISO/IEC 42005-aligned) for RT3/RT4 participation before an autonomy grant, feeding risk-tiering and autonomy limits.
- **Crosswalk currency: agentic governance + EU AI Act timeline** (adversarially verified, 24/25 claims confirmed by the standards-landscape review): §1 adds the *Levels of Autonomy for AI Agents* five user-role taxonomy (Operator→…→Observer) and the "autonomy is a design choice independent of capability" principle; §2 adds the dated EU AI Act status (GPAI obligations live 2 Aug 2025, enforcement 2 Aug 2026, GPAI Code of Practice 10 Jul 2025, CEN-CENELEC harmonized standards targeted Q4 2026 under accelerated procedures); §3b dates the OWASP Agentic Top 10 (ASI01–ASI10, 9 Dec 2025) and adds AAGATE (NIST-RMF agentic control plane); a positioning row for AI eval benchmarks/frameworks (AILuminate, Inspect, DeepEval, HELM) as complementary. Added an **edition caveat** that the EV1–EV6 ↔ 25059 mapping tracks the 2023 edition (the 2nd edition, DIS ~2026, moves *intervenability* under a new Safety characteristic).
- **Crosswalk to ISO/IEC SC 42 technical standards** ([docs/CROSSWALK.md §3a](docs/CROSSWALK.md)): maps the AIES engineering spine to the AI *technical* standards family (distinct from the 42001 management layer already covered) — **ISO/IEC TS 42119-2:2025** (Testing of AI; applies the ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119 series to AI) ↔ AESQS + the platform's risk-based scenario testing; **25059** (AI quality model over SQuaRE/25010) ↔ a new **EV1–EV6 ↔ 25059** characteristic mapping; **5338** (AI life-cycle processes) ↔ SDLC phases P01–P16; **42005:2025** (impact assessment) ↔ AEOS; **42006:2025** (certification bodies) ↔ AECT. Adds a note on anchoring AIES scenarios/gates to NIST GenAI Profile action IDs (e.g. `GV-1.1-001`). Sourced from a live-web standards-landscape review (Jul 2026); core facts adversarially verified (ISO 42119-2 published 3 Nov 2025; 42005 published 28 May 2025; NIST AI 600-1 twelve GenAI risks + action-ID scheme).

- **Judge views — `aies judge available` / `judge list` / `judge history`**: see the judge *pool* and the judge *track record*. `judge available` lists and **counts** the deployments you have registered as judges (those declaring the new advisory `roles: [judge]` manifest tag), each annotated with its track record or "never used". `judge list` aggregates per judge deployment across runs it actually scored (runs judged, responses scored, **parse rate**, self-judged-run count, whether still registered); `judge history` lists one row per judged run (subject, judge, scored/responses, unparseable, aggregated or not), optionally filtered to a single judge. The `list`/`history` views are derived entirely from the `model`-kind rating records already written by `qualify --judge` — nothing new is stored. Parse rate exposes a judge that frequently emits unparseable scores — thin evidence a capability number alone would hide. Deployment manifests gain an optional `roles` field (`subject` and/or `judge`, advisory, validated but not enforced; distinct from the Taxonomy's organizational ROLE-01…14); the bundled judge examples are tagged accordingly.
- **Parallel judge scoring + visible concurrency**: `--parallel N` now applies to **both** phases of `qualify --judge` — collecting the candidate's answers *and* the judge scoring every response. Previously the judge scored strictly one response at a time regardless of `--parallel`, so a `--judge` run was serial for its entire second half; it is now concurrent end to end (results still ingested in canonical order). Both phases print how many workers they use (`collecting responses across N worker(s)…`, `scoring M responses … across N worker(s)…`) so the concurrency is observable. Surfaced by first-real-run feedback ("I doubt requests are still running in sequence").
- **Judge-deployment examples ([platform/examples/deployments/](platform/examples/deployments/README.md))**: copy-paste manifests for running the judge as a hosted frontier model, a second model on the same machine, or a model on another machine on the LAN — "cloud vs local vs remote" is only the `base_url`. Any registered `openai-compat` deployment can be the judge. Includes ready-made frontier-judge manifests (Claude Opus 4.8 / Sonnet 5 / Fable 5 via Anthropic's OpenAI-compatibility endpoint; GPT-5.6 Sol / Terra / Luna via OpenAI), all tagged `roles: [judge]`.
- **Run transcript — `aies transcript <run>`**: renders a whole run in one readable document — per scenario (and repeat) the task, the model's answer, its expected qualities, and the scores/findings inline — so a reviewer reads or spot-checks a run top to bottom instead of opening dozens of per-response JSON files. Markdown or JSON, optional `--area` filter, `--write` to save `transcript.md`. Response records now also store the full prompt text (not just a hash) so the transcript is self-contained (and journeys show the actual assembled prompt). Surfaced by first-real-run feedback ("I have to go to and fro to verify each output").
- **Automated scoring — `aies qualify --judge <deployment>`** (and `$AIES_JUDGE` default): run a qualification end to end and get a **scored report directly**, with no manual scoresheet step. A judge model rates every response 0–4 on EV1–EV6; the run aggregates and the report prints. The report carries a **JUDGE-PRODUCED** banner (scores are the judge's opinion, not ground truth), stays evidence-only (a grant remains an optional human step, D8), and warns loudly on self-judging (candidate scoring its own output). Manual human scoring remains available by omitting `--judge`. Reuses the calibration-gated model-reviewer machinery. Surfaced by first real-run feedback: "we ran qualify and the result should be output, not next steps."

- **Continuous Improvement & Maintenance note** ([docs/IMPROVEMENT.md](docs/IMPROVEMENT.md), AIES-DOC-09): the data flywheel by which AIES improves itself — real runs → signals (item analysis, drift, rater disagreement, cross-model clustering, incidents, errata) → human curation / re-qualification / ADR → versioned change — with the firm line that the normative core (gates, weights, minimums, autonomy caps, taxonomy) changes *only* through a human ADR, never automatically. Includes the maintenance cadence (continuous / per-release / quarterly / annual / trigger-based), version-increment rules, and a deprecation policy. Linked from GOVERNANCE and AEBOK KA-12. Explicitly design-only: the machinery (e.g. `aies analyze` item analysis) is to be built once real runs exist, not before.
- **Conformance-claim mechanism** ([docs/CONFORMANCE.md](docs/CONFORMANCE.md), AIES-DOC-08; `aies conform`): defines what it means to conform to AIES — two classes (implementation / adopter), three assurance levels (self-asserted / evidence-backed / independently-reviewed), and a versioned conformance statement — and makes it checkable. `aies conform template` scaffolds a statement, `aies conform requirements` lists the normative requirements the reference implementation enforces (with where), and `aies conform check` validates a statement and verifies evidence-backed claims against live Qualification Records (exiting non-zero, for CI, when an evidence-backed statement is not substantiated). Honest by design: the checker verifies statement structure and record existence/status — not the truth of a qualification or a reviewer's independence. This is what turns AIES from a framework into a standard others can conform to.
- **Standards crosswalk** ([docs/CROSSWALK.md](docs/CROSSWALK.md), AIES-DOC-07): maps AIES's autonomy levels (AL0–AL4) and risk tiers (RT1–RT4) to the CSA *Levels of Autonomy for Agentic AI*, the ASDLC/AI-SDLC autonomy scale, and the EU AI Act; maps AIES modules/artifacts to NIST AI RMF functions and ISO/IEC 42001; and positions AEBOK/AECT relative to ARTiBA AMDEX and CertNexus CAIP. Reframes AIES as the executable qualification layer that plugs into existing governance frameworks rather than competing with them, and states the gap AIES uniquely fills. Mappings are explicitly approximate, one-directional aids — not conformance or endorsement. Linked from the FAQ and the Taxonomy.
- **Discoverable CLI help**: bare `aies` (and `aies help`) now prints the full command map grouped by stage (setup & discovery / qualification / decision & audit / reference), a copy-pasteable end-to-end workflow, **and a full command tree** — every command *and* subcommand (e.g. `profile list`, `qualification history`, `conform check/template/requirements`) with its usage line, introspected from the live parser so it never drifts — instead of erroring; `aies <command> --help` details each command. The usage line is decluttered (a `<command>` placeholder rather than the full brace list).
- **Multi-phase journeys** (`aies journey list/show`, `aies qualify --journey <id>`): a new scenario type that carries one piece of work *through* the SDLC — each step's response is threaded into the next step's prompt — testing lifecycle depth, not just breadth. Steps are recorded as ordinary response records tagged with their competency area, so the entire existing pipeline (score → aggregate → report → grant → verify) consumes journeys unchanged. Ships JOURNEY-01 (requirement → architecture → implementation → tests → release/oversight) and JOURNEY-02 (incident diagnosis → fix → regression test → post-incident governance), plus JOURNEYS.md (AIES-PLAT-05). A single journey is depth not volume, so it renders NON-DECISIONAL on its own by design.
- **Qualification reports** (`aies report QUAL-… [--format md|json|html] [--write]`, `aies report list`): durable, auditor-facing report artifacts rendered from a Qualification Record — the human decision, scoped grant (per-area CL + RT×AL envelope), accountable humans, conditions, status, and the environment it was earned against — written to a listable `reports/` area separate from run directories. Self-contained HTML (no external requests). `aies report <run>` (evidence-package report) is unchanged.
- **Model-reviewer execution** (`aies review --model-reviewer <deployment>`): the platform now drives a reviewer deployment to critique and score each candidate response (structured EV1–EV6 output, parsed best-effort — an item that can't be parsed into six 0–4 integers is skipped and reported, never fabricated), ingests the results as `model`-kind ratings, and applies the calibration gate. Completes the M4 peer-review loop as an execution framework rather than a hand-authored scoresheet.
- **`aies runtime` resource command + RUNTIMES.md** (AIES-PLAT-04): runtime is now a first-class resource — `aies runtime list` shows every adapter, its contract version, and availability; `aies runtime inspect <id>` shows capabilities, the endpoint env var + conventional default, and models served now. RUNTIMES.md documents the frozen v1.0 runtime interface, the six shipped adapters, and how to add one out-of-tree.
- **Resource-model CLI + qualification manifests**: noun-verb commands `aies deployment` (list/inspect/add/retire), `aies profile` (list/show/validate), and `aies qualification` (list/show/history/verify/revoke) as the primary surface, alongside the existing verbs. Qualification records now carry human-friendly sequential ids `QUAL-YYYY-NNN` and are enriched into a self-contained audit manifest (deployment, model, runtime, checksum, scope, evidence, environment fingerprint, humans, status). New platform docs: `PROFILES.md` (AIES-PLAT-02) and `DEPLOYMENTS.md` (AIES-PLAT-03, "qualify deployments, not models"). Fix: the `architect` profile weighted CA-03 (Product & Experience) instead of CA-04 (Architecture & Solution Design) — a stale weight from before ADR-0003.
- **Built-in adapters for common local runtimes** — `ollama`, `lmstudio`, `llamacpp`, and `mlx` (thin subclasses over the OpenAI-compatible HTTP adapter), each auto-detected on its conventional port and overridable via its own `AIES_<RUNTIME>_BASE_URL`. `aies doctor` reports all runtimes; `aies discover` registers whatever each serves. Configurable probe timeout (`AIES_PROBE_TIMEOUT_S`). The platform GUIDE gains per-runtime setup and a start-to-finish worked run.
- **Platform configuration via env vars / `.env`** (`config.py`, `.env.example`): all operational settings — workspace path, `openai-compat` endpoint and API key, request timeout, parallelism, and generation defaults (temperature/max-tokens/top-p/seed) — are read from the environment or a git-ignored `.env` file, with a dependency-free loader (precedence: CLI/manifest → real env → `.env` → default). The hard-coded `localhost:11434` endpoint default was removed (unset now means "runtime not configured"). Normative engine constants (gates, weights, sample minimums, confidence, calibration threshold) remain deliberately non-configurable (D3), with a test enforcing it.
- **Platform Milestone 4 — review & workflow** (completes the platform): multi-model peer-review orchestration (`aies review`) with the reviewer-model **calibration gate** (an unqualified, uncalibrated reviewer's scores are advisory only) and divergence surfacing (≥2-point or gate-changing disagreements are listed for human resolution, never averaged); the **human grant workflow** (`aies grant`) producing a **Qualification Record** — a named human authority records every decision, a second human is required to grant, and grants on non-decisional or gate-failing evidence are refused (the platform never grants); the **status model** (active/conditional/denied/invalidated/revoked/superseded) with `aies verify` enforcing the **environment-change re-qualification trigger** (D7 — a changed fingerprint invalidates a grant, non-zero exit for CI); `aies qualifications` (list/show/revoke); an HTML `aies dashboard`; and a GitHub Actions CI workflow running the conformance suite on Python 3.10–3.12. Platform milestones M1–M4 complete.

### Added

- **`aies deployment update` / `aies deployment remove`**: fixing a registered deployment no longer means hand-editing a workspace file or juggling ids. `update <file>` overwrites an existing entry in place (same id) — for a wrong endpoint, an `api_key_env` typo, changed generation defaults, or adding `roles: [judge]` — and **warns** when a behavioral-identity field (runtime, model, quantization, `runtime_config`) changed, since prior qualifications for that id may no longer describe what runs (the D7 fingerprint catches it on `verify`). `remove <id>` hard-deletes an entry so its id is free to reuse, as a counterpart to `retire` (which soft-marks and keeps the id reserved for audit). `add` remains new-ids-only. Surfaced by first-real-run friction (fixing a mis-registered judge required deleting the stored YAML by hand).

- **Troubleshooting guide ([platform/TROUBLESHOOTING.md](platform/TROUBLESHOOTING.md), AIES-PLAT-06)**: symptom → cause → fix for the issues that actually come up against real endpoints — timeouts, `CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED`, `401/403/404/429`, slow runs (`max_tokens`/`--repeats`), `--parallel` on local GPUs, re-scoring a collected run after a judge failure, `already registered` (use `update`), and low judge parse rates. Linked from the platform README, GUIDE §5.2, and the `aies help` epilog. GUIDE also gained the re-score-without-recollect flow and a performance note.

### Fixed

- **HTTPS to cloud judges/endpoints now verifies certificates reliably.** On a freshly-installed Python (notably python.org macOS builds) an HTTPS call failed with `CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED — unable to get local issuer certificate`. The `openai-compat` adapter now builds its TLS context from **certifi's** CA bundle (added as a dependency), while still honoring an explicit `SSL_CERT_FILE`/`SSL_CERT_DIR` (corporate CA). TLS-verification failures also get a specific remediation hint instead of being lumped in with timeouts, and timeouts no longer misreport a fast failure as "within 300s". `aies review --model-reviewer` gained `--parallel` (and a worker banner), so re-scoring an already-collected run with a judge is concurrent — you can re-run just the judge step against saved responses instead of re-collecting.
- **Endpoint failures are now actionable, and never lose collected work.** A request timeout previously surfaced as a bare `error: timed out` with no indication of which endpoint or why (a raw `TimeoutError` slipped past the adapter's `URLError`-only handler on Python 3.14). The `openai-compat` adapter now names the endpoint and the timeout, distinguishes HTTP status errors (401/404/429 with guidance) from unreachable hosts, and suggests `AIES_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_S` / lowering `--parallel`. The pre-run **liveness (smoke) probe** now fails fast (≤30 s) instead of hanging for the full 300 s request timeout when an endpoint is dead or misconfigured. And `execute_suite` now **writes each response as it completes** and raises a single summary error on failure — so a timeout partway through no longer discards the responses already collected (previously a parallel batch was all-or-nothing). Surfaced by first-real-run feedback (`collecting responses across 8 worker(s).. error: timed out`).
